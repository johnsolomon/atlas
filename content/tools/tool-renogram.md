A bilateral teaching bench for the ⁹⁹ᵐTc-MAG3 / DTPA dynamic renogram. Drag either kidney's curve — its perfusion upstroke, cortical peak (T<sub>max</sub>), and drainage tail — and the panel decodes it live: the three phases, washout T½, output efficiency and NORA, relative (split) function, the diuretic (furosemide) response, and the O'Reilly pattern. A separate flow-phase strip and a paired ACE-inhibitor (captopril) module show how the study is actually read across its two acquisitions, and the reference gallery loads classic drainage patterns onto the curve for comparison.

Read it the way you would a real study: the curve is only one axis — always pair it with relative function and the images. A prolonged washout T½ is **not** diagnostic of obstruction on its own (dehydration, poor function, a capacious reservoir, and bladder backpressure all prolong it), which is why the tool foregrounds quantitative drainage indices (output efficiency, NORA) and the diuretic response rather than the raw half-time. And renovascular disease is defined by the **change after the ACE-inhibitor**, not the resting curve.
