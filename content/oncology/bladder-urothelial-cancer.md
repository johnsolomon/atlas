## Quick Answer

FDG is **renally excreted**, so intense urinary activity in the bladder and collecting systems **obscures the primary tumor** — the defining challenge of imaging bladder and upper-tract urothelial carcinoma. FDG-PET/CT is therefore **not the primary local-staging tool** (cystoscopy, MRI, and CT urography assess the primary and bladder wall), but it is valuable for **nodal and distant staging in muscle-invasive/high-risk disease, restaging, and treatment response**, where its accuracy exceeds CT alone. Mitigation of the urinary pitfall — **hydration, delayed imaging, furosemide, and/or bladder catheterisation/irrigation** — is what makes pelvic assessment interpretable.

## The urinary-excretion pitfall

{K} Because FDG is **excreted in urine**, the bladder is intensely "hot" and the ureters and renal pelvis retain activity — this can **hide a bladder-wall tumor** and mimic or mask ureteric/nodal disease. This single fact governs urothelial PET: the primary tumor is the least reliable thing to assess, and any focal pelvic uptake must be separated from pooled urinary activity before it is called disease.

{H} Techniques to unmask disease: **oral hydration and a diuretic (furosemide) with delayed imaging** to wash out and dilute urinary activity; **bladder emptying/catheterisation or retrograde irrigation** in dedicated protocols; and correlating any focus with the CT and with delayed views. Without these, a bladder primary and juxtavesical nodes are unreliable.

## Where PET adds value

{H} FDG-PET/CT is most useful **beyond the primary**:

- **Nodal and distant staging** in **muscle-invasive/high-risk** disease — more sensitive/specific than CT for nodal and metastatic disease, changing management in a meaningful fraction.
- **Restaging and suspected recurrence**, including after cystectomy or chemotherapy.
- **Treatment response** (neoadjuvant chemotherapy, immunotherapy) — with the usual caveat that post-therapy inflammation and immune-related effects can confound.
- **Upper-tract urothelial carcinoma** metastatic workup.

{D} Urothelial carcinoma is generally **FDG-avid**, so metabolic assessment is informative once the urinary pitfall is managed. Uptake in a **thickened bladder wall** that persists on delayed, post-void or post-furosemide imaging (i.e. not pooled urine) supports tumour.

## Pitfalls and cautions

- {H} **Do not call bladder-wall or pelvic uptake malignant without excluding urinary contamination** — use delayed/diuretic/post-void imaging and CT correlation.
- **Post-treatment inflammation** (BCG for non-muscle-invasive disease, chemoradiation) is FDG-avid — timing matters.
- **Physiologic ureteric activity** and a distended bladder can obscure or mimic nodal disease along the pelvic sidewalls.

## Common Pitfalls / Cautions

- FDG-PET is not the primary local-staging tool for the bladder wall (cystoscopy/MRI/CT urography are).
- Always interpret the pelvis with diuretic/delayed technique when assessing urothelial disease.
