## Quick Answer

FDG-PET/CT detects peritoneal metastatic disease — from **ovarian, colorectal, gastric, appendiceal, and other** primaries — and is valuable for **staging, restaging, and selecting candidates for cytoreductive surgery ± HIPEC** by finding disease that changes operability. Its two important limits are **small-volume (miliary) peritoneal deposits below PET resolution** and **low-grade mucinous disease (e.g. pseudomyxoma peritonei)**, which is often **FDG-poor** — both can be underestimated. Physiologic bowel activity and adjacent organ uptake further complicate the crowded abdomen, so PET is read alongside contrast CT/MRI and, where it matters, diagnostic laparoscopy.

## What PET sees — and misses

{K} FDG-PET/CT is **sensitive for macroscopic, metabolically active peritoneal disease** (nodular implants, omental caking, avid ascites-associated nodules) and detects extra-abdominal and nodal disease that alters management. But it is **limited for small-volume miliary spread below resolution** and for **low-grade mucinous carcinomatosis**, which is characteristically **FDG-poor** — so a negative or low-uptake study does not exclude peritoneal disease, especially in mucinous histologies.

{H} High-yield points: **omental caking and nodular peritoneal implants** are the classic avid findings; **mucinous/low-grade disease (pseudomyxoma peritonei, mucinous appendiceal/ovarian tumours)** is a recognized **false negative**; and **physiologic bowel FDG** (peristalsis, lymphoid tissue, metformin) must be separated from implants — delayed views and CT correlation help.

## Where it changes management

{D} For **cytoreductive surgery with HIPEC**, the **peritoneal cancer index (PCI)** and resectability drive selection; PET/CT contributes by detecting **extraperitoneal or bulky disease** that would preclude complete cytoreduction, while **laparoscopy** remains the reference for the true peritoneal burden because of PET's small-volume limitation. In **ovarian cancer recurrence** with rising CA-125, FDG-PET frequently localizes peritoneal and nodal recurrence when conventional imaging is equivocal.

## Common Pitfalls / Cautions

- {H} A negative FDG-PET does not exclude **small-volume** or **mucinous** peritoneal disease.
- Physiologic bowel and urinary activity mimic or obscure implants — correlate with CT/delayed imaging.
- PET complements but does not replace laparoscopy for peritoneal burden before cytoreduction.

## Board Pearls

- {K} The classic avid findings are **omental caking** and **nodular peritoneal/serosal implants**; PET adds value chiefly by finding **extraperitoneal, nodal, or bulky disease** that changes operability.
- {H} Two classic **false negatives**: **small-volume miliary spread** below PET resolution and **low-grade mucinous disease** (pseudomyxoma peritonei, mucinous appendiceal/ovarian tumors).
- Common primaries are **ovarian** (most frequent), **colorectal, gastric, and appendiceal**.
- FDG-PET is particularly useful in **ovarian cancer recurrence with rising CA-125** when conventional imaging is equivocal.
- **Diagnostic laparoscopy** remains the reference standard for true peritoneal burden (**PCI**) before cytoreductive surgery/HIPEC.

## Differential Diagnosis

- **Tuberculous or other infectious peritonitis** — FDG-avid peritoneal/omental thickening and ascites that closely mimics carcinomatosis.
- **Peritoneal mesothelioma** and **primary peritoneal carcinoma**.
- **Post-surgical or reactive inflammation**, dialysis-related peritonitis, and **endometriosis**.
- **Physiologic bowel and omental-fat FDG** simulating implants.
- Other avid processes such as **splenosis, desmoid, or talc/suture granulomas**.

## Reporting Checklist

- Report **distribution** (omental caking, implants in subphrenic spaces, paracolic gutters, pouch of Douglas) and any ascites.
- State the **small-volume/mucinous limitation** — a negative or low-uptake study does not exclude peritoneal disease.
- Identify **extraperitoneal, nodal, or bulky disease** affecting resectability/complete cytoreduction.
- Separate **physiologic bowel/urinary activity** using CT correlation and delayed views.
- Correlate with **contrast CT/MRI** and recommend **laparoscopy** where peritoneal burden is decision-critical.
