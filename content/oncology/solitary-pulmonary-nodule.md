## Quick Answer

For an **indeterminate solid pulmonary nodule** (roughly ≥8–10 mm), FDG-PET/CT refines the pre-test probability of malignancy: **higher uptake → higher malignancy risk**, and a metabolically **negative** solid nodule has a high enough negative predictive value to support surveillance rather than biopsy. But the test is defined by its **false positives** (infection/inflammation and granulomatous disease are avid) and **false negatives** (carcinoid, minimally-invasive/lepidic adenocarcinoma, and sub-centimetre or ground-glass nodules can be quiet). A rigid **SUVmax 2.5 threshold** is a teaching heuristic, not a rule — interpret uptake against nodule size, morphology, growth, and the patient's infection/granuloma risk.

## How to use uptake

{K} FDG uptake shifts the malignancy probability rather than diagnosing: an intensely avid solid nodule in a smoker is high-risk, while a **metabolically negative solid nodule ≥1 cm** has a high negative predictive value and can often be followed. The classic **SUVmax ≈ 2.5** cut-off is a **rough heuristic** — sensitivity/specificity trade off around it, and small nodules are under-measured by partial-volume effects, so uptake is always read **with size, morphology (spiculation, growth), and clinical risk**, not in isolation.

## The false positives

{H} Anything metabolically active mimics malignancy: **active infection and inflammation** (pneumonia, abscess), **granulomatous disease** — **tuberculosis, histoplasmosis, coccidioidomycosis, sarcoidosis** — **rheumatoid nodules**, and **organizing pneumonia**. In regions endemic for granulomatous disease this substantially lowers PET specificity, and a hot nodule there may still be benign. This is the single biggest limitation of FDG for the SPN.

## The false negatives

{H} Low-grade or low-cellularity tumours can be FDG-quiet: **typical carcinoid**, **minimally invasive / lepidic-predominant adenocarcinoma** (formerly BAC), and **ground-glass or part-solid nodules**, plus **sub-centimetre nodules** (partial-volume underestimation).

{K} A **negative FDG-PET does not exclude malignancy in a ground-glass, part-solid, or sub-centimetre nodule** — these are followed by CT criteria, not cleared by a cold PET.

## Putting it together

{D} FDG-PET is most useful for the **solid, indeterminate nodule of intermediate pre-test probability**: it can raise or lower risk enough to change the decision between surveillance, biopsy, and resection, and it simultaneously screens for **nodal and distant disease** if malignancy is likely. It is **not** the right test for pure ground-glass lesions (follow by CT) and does not replace tissue when suspicion remains despite low uptake.

## Common Pitfalls / Cautions

- {H} A hot nodule in a granuloma-endemic area may be benign (infection/granuloma); a cold ground-glass/sub-cm nodule may be malignant.
- SUVmax 2.5 is a heuristic, not a threshold to apply mechanically.
- Interpret uptake with size, morphology, growth, and infection/granuloma risk.

## Board Pearls

- {K} FDG uptake **shifts malignancy probability** rather than diagnosing — a metabolically **negative solid nodule ≥1 cm** has a high negative predictive value supporting surveillance; higher uptake raises risk.
- {H} The single biggest limitation is **granulomatous/infectious false positives**, which sharply lower specificity in endemic regions.
- Classic **false negatives**: **typical carcinoid**, **lepidic/minimally invasive adenocarcinoma**, and **ground-glass, part-solid, or sub-centimetre** nodules.
- A **hamartoma** may be mildly avid; look for **macroscopic fat and "popcorn" calcification** on CT to call it benign.
- When malignancy is likely, PET simultaneously provides **nodal and distant staging** — but it is **not** the test for pure ground-glass lesions (follow by CT).

## Differential Diagnosis

- **Primary lung cancer** (adenocarcinoma, squamous) or a **solitary metastasis** — the malignant targets.
- **Infectious granuloma** — tuberculosis, histoplasmosis, coccidioidomycosis, aspergilloma.
- **Active infection/inflammation** — focal pneumonia, abscess, septic embolus.
- **Inflammatory nodules** — organizing pneumonia, rheumatoid nodule, sarcoidosis.
- **Benign neoplasm** — hamartoma (fat/popcorn calcification), rounded atelectasis.
- Remember FDG-quiet malignancies (carcinoid, lepidic adenocarcinoma, ground-glass/sub-cm) are **not excluded by low uptake**.

## Reporting Checklist

- Report **SUVmax with nodule size and morphology** (spiculation, calcification pattern, growth vs priors) — never uptake alone.
- Integrate **infection/granuloma risk** and endemic exposure into the probability estimate.
- State that **cold ground-glass, part-solid, or sub-cm nodules are not cleared** — follow by CT (Fleischner) criteria.
- Recommend **biopsy or resection** when suspicion persists despite low uptake.
- Comment on **nodal and distant staging** when malignancy is likely.
