## Quick Answer

In an **immunocompromised patient (classically AIDS) with a ring-enhancing brain lesion**, the central question is **primary CNS lymphoma (PCNSL) versus toxoplasmosis** — treated completely differently (chemotherapy/radiation vs anti-parasitic therapy). Functional imaging separates them by metabolism: **PCNSL is hypermetabolic — FDG-avid and thallium-201-avid**, while **toxoplasmosis is an infection/abscess that is typically non-avid (cold) on thallium and low on FDG**. So an **avid lesion favors lymphoma** and a **non-avid lesion favors toxoplasmosis**, complementing the MRI and clinical context (Toxoplasma serology, response to empiric anti-toxoplasma therapy).

## The metabolic discriminator

{K} **PCNSL is a hypercellular, highly metabolic tumour** → **increased FDG uptake and increased thallium-201 uptake**. **Toxoplasmosis is an inflammatory/abscess process** → **little thallium uptake and generally low FDG**. Thus **increased uptake favours lymphoma; absent/low uptake favours toxoplasmosis**. Thallium-201 brain SPECT was the classic tool for exactly this question; FDG-PET serves the same role with better resolution.

{H} Supporting points: a **single lesion, periventricular/subependymal location, and subependymal spread** favour lymphoma; **multiple lesions and a positive Toxoplasma serology** favour toxoplasmosis. Empiric anti-toxoplasma therapy with **follow-up imaging** is a standard confirmatory step — lack of response raises suspicion for lymphoma and prompts biopsy.

## Caveats

{D} The distinction is **probabilistic, not absolute**: other infections and **treatment effects** can confound, steroids can transiently shrink lymphoma ("ghost tumour") and reduce avidity, and small or necrotic lesions are harder to characterise. Functional imaging **raises or lowers the probability** and guides whether to pursue empiric anti-toxoplasma treatment versus biopsy — it does not replace tissue when management hinges on certainty.

## Common Pitfalls / Cautions

- {H} **Steroids can mask lymphoma** (shrinking it and reducing uptake) — imaging after steroids may underestimate disease.
- Necrotic/treated lesions and coexisting infection blur the pattern.
- Interpret with MRI, serology, and response to empiric therapy — not uptake alone.

## Board Pearls

- {K} In AIDS the two dominant ring-enhancing lesions are **toxoplasmosis** (most common) and **PCNSL**; because MRI appearances overlap, functional imaging is the key noninvasive discriminator.
- {H} Adding **CSF EBV DNA** to thallium/FDG avidity increases specificity for PCNSL in the immunocompromised.
- **Thallium-201 delayed retention** characterizes lymphoma; thallium brain SPECT was the classic tool, now largely replaced by higher-resolution FDG-PET.
- AIDS-related PCNSL is nearly always **EBV-driven diffuse large B-cell lymphoma**; whole-body FDG-PET screens for systemic lymphoma that would change management.

## Differential Diagnosis

- **Toxoplasmosis** — typically multiple lesions at the basal ganglia/corticomedullary junction with positive serology; non-avid.
- **PCNSL** — often a single periventricular/subependymal avid lesion.
- **Other opportunistic infections** — tuberculoma, cryptococcoma, and bacterial/fungal abscess (generally low uptake, though granulomas can be variably avid).
- **Progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML)** — typically non-enhancing white-matter disease, a distinct pattern.
- **Metastasis or other primary tumor** — considered when the clinical setting is not classic AIDS.

## Reporting Checklist

- State **lesion number and location** (periventricular/subependymal vs basal ganglia) and whether solitary or multiple.
- Report **FDG/thallium uptake** relative to normal cortex/background and give a lymphoma-versus-toxoplasmosis probability, not a definitive call.
- Note **steroid exposure and timing**, which may mask lymphoma.
- Integrate **Toxoplasma serology, CD4 count, and CSF EBV** where available.
- Recommend the **next step** — empiric anti-toxoplasma therapy with follow-up imaging versus biopsy.
